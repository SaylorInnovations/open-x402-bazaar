# Evidence-based prospect research

Use this skill to build a small, defensible prospect list for a defined offer, market, and buyer. Read the existing campaign notes and pipeline first; current user instructions take precedence over saved plans.

## Research and qualification

1. Define the target business type, service area, offer, exclusions, and existing do-not-contact rules before searching.
2. Use current public sources. Save each source URL and the date checked; prefer first-party business pages and other direct evidence.
3. Record observable facts separately from inferences. Do not invent businesses, owner names, contact details, reviews, defects, budgets, or expected results.
4. A failed fetch does not prove a site is broken. Retry using another suitable method or mark the claim unverified.
5. Deduplicate by domain and other reliable identifiers. Exclude opt-outs and prior suppressions from outreach candidates.
6. Return fewer prospects when the available evidence does not support the requested quantity.

## Drafting and tracking

For each qualified prospect, record the evidence, why it fits the defined offer, public contact route, and a concise draft based on one verified observation. Do not present a public estimate as an approved quote or imply results that have not been demonstrated.

Keep status precise: research is not outreach, a draft is not sent, a quote is not won, and a win is not necessarily paid. Preserve contact history and opt-outs. Read existing records before editing and preserve unrelated content.

Research and drafting do not authorize email, direct messages, calls, form submissions, paid tools, publishing, or scheduled campaigns. Follow the user's current authorization and the available platform controls. Report what was actually researched, saved, sent, or left unverified.
